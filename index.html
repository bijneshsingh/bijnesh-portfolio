I'm Bijnesh, a Principal Product Designer based in Hyderabad, with user research at the core of how I work. I started in web and graphic design, building websites, campaigns and HTML mailers for brands like Indiabulls, Investors Clinic and Amrapali Group. That gave me a strong visual and front‑end foundation, and over the years my work moved from screens to systems: UX strategy, information architecture, design systems and end‑to‑end product design across AI SaaS, fintech, media and e‑commerce.

For the last few years I have focused on AI products. At BuzzBoard I worked on Zylo5, an agentic AI marketing platform, where the core design question was trust: how do you let an AI act for a business owner while keeping the owner in control? That shaped how I design today. Clear approval steps, honest status and error states, and a reason beside every suggestion.

I also use AI in my own process. Tools like Claude, Figma MCP, Google Stitch, Lovable and Replit let me go from idea to a working concept quickly, so teams can react to something real early. The design decisions stay mine.

To take that further, I completed Vibe Coding 101 with Replit from DeepLearning.AI. It taught me to build full‑stack web apps with an AI agent: write a clear product spec, prompt in small steps, test and debug what the agent builds, and deploy. For a designer, that means I can hand a team a working prototype, not just a Figma file, and I understand what it takes for developers to build what I design.

How I work

A process that fits the problem, not the other way round

Whether it is a fintech app, a media microsite or a SaaS platform, I follow the same core steps. The depth of each step changes with the project, the timeline and the team.

Step 1

Understand

Talk to stakeholders and users, review analytics and run UX audits to learn the business goal, who the users are and where the current experience fails them.

Step 2

Define

Turn findings into a clear problem statement, user journeys and success measures, so the team agrees on what we are solving before anyone opens Figma.

Step 3

Structure

Information architecture, task flows and low‑fidelity wireframes, covering edge cases, empty states and errors as well as the happy path.

Step 4

Design and prototype

High‑fidelity UI built on a design system, with clickable prototypes. I use AI tools to explore options quickly, then refine by hand, and when a flow needs real data I vibe code a working prototype.

Step 5

Test and iterate

Usability tests with real users, then revise. Small, frequent rounds catch problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Step 6

Hand off and measure

Developer‑ready specs and components, design QA during build, and a look at the numbers after launch to plan the next round.
